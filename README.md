# VolGAN++: Conditional GAN for Volatility Surface Modeling and Portfolio Optimization

## 1. Introduction

VolGAN++ is a Python script that implements an advanced Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) for modeling financial volatility surfaces. It incorporates Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) principles by enforcing financial market constraints such as no-arbitrage conditions, VIX replication, and consistency with the Dupire equation. Beyond surface generation, VolGAN++ integrates a portfolio optimization module that learns to construct Sharpe-optimal portfolios based on the generated volatility surfaces.

The model is designed with a conceptual focus on intraday dynamics, although the current mock data generation is simplified. It demonstrates how GANs can be conditioned on market state variables (spot price, VIX index, realized volatility) to produce realistic and actionable financial data.

## 2. Core Components

The script is organized into several key parts:

### 2.1. Synthetic Intraday Data Generation (`generate_mock_data`)

This function simulates market data required for training.
- **Inputs:** `batch_size`, `surface_shape` (e.g., (1, 32, 32) for 1 channel, 32 strikes, 32 maturities), `asset_dim` (number of assets for portfolio).
- **Outputs:**
    - `state_t`: Current market state (spot price, VIX, realized volatility). Spot is ~4500, VIX is 10-25%, realized vol is 20-30%.
    - `surface_t`: Mock current volatility surface (not directly used in training G, but for context).
    - `surface_t1`: Target next-period volatility surface, derived from `surface_t` with injected skew and noise. This is the "real" data for the discriminator.
    - `returns_t1`: Target next-period asset returns.
    - `prev_weights`: Previous period's portfolio weights.
    - `target_vix`: Target next-period VIX value.
    - `strikes`: Grid of strike prices for the volatility surface (e.g., 4000-5000).
    - `maturities`: Grid of maturities for the volatility surface (e.g., 1 day to 1 year).

### 2.2. Model Definitions

Three neural network models are defined:

#### a. `ConditionalForwardGenerator` (G)
- **Purpose:** Generates the next-period volatility surface (`fake_surface`) conditioned on the current market state (`state_t`) and a noise vector (`z`).
- **Architecture:**
    - Input: Concatenation of noise vector `z` (latent dimension, e.g., 16) and `state_t` (dimension 3: normalized spot, VIX, realized vol).
    - `fc`: Fully connected layer mapping input to 128 features.
    - `net`: A sequential network of `Softplus` activations and `Linear` layers, ultimately outputting a flattened version of the surface. The final activation within this sequence is `Softplus` to ensure positive volatility values.
    - The `forward` method scales the output of `self.net` by a factor (e.g., 0.25) to help control the magnitude of generated implied volatilities, ensuring they align better with typical market levels (e.g., 0.1-0.3).
- **Output Shape:** Defined by `output_shape`, e.g., (1, 32, 32).

#### b. `Discriminator` (D)
- **Purpose:** Distinguishes between real target volatility surfaces (`surface_t1`) and fake surfaces generated by `G`. It is also conditioned on `state_t`.
- **Architecture:**
    - Input: Concatenation of a flattened volatility surface and `state_t_normalized`.
    - `net`: A sequence of `Linear` layers with `LeakyReLU` activations, outputting a single scalar (the "realness" score).

#### c. `SharpeOptimizingPortfolio` (P)
- **Purpose:** Learns to construct an optimal portfolio by outputting asset weights, based on an input volatility surface (typically the `fake_surface` from G).
- **Architecture:**
    - Input: A flattened volatility surface.
    - `model`: A sequence of `Linear` layers with `ReLU` and a final `Softmax` activation to ensure weights sum to 1 and are non-negative.
- **Output:** Portfolio weights for `asset_dim` assets.

### 2.3. Loss Functions

Several loss functions are defined to train the models and enforce financial constraints:

#### a. GAN Loss (Implicit)
- Calculated within the training loop.
- **Discriminator Loss (`loss_D`):** Standard GAN loss: `-D(real_surface).mean() + D(fake_surface).mean()`. Aims to maximize the score for real surfaces and minimize it for fake ones.
- **Generator GAN Loss (`loss_gan`):** `-D(fake_surface).mean()`. Aims to generate surfaces that the discriminator scores as highly as real ones.

#### b. `sharpe_loss(weights, returns)`
- **Purpose:** Encourages the portfolio `P` to maximize the Sharpe ratio.
- **Calculation:** `- (mean_portfolio_returns / std_portfolio_returns)`. The negative is taken because optimizers minimize loss. A small epsilon is added to the standard deviation for numerical stability.

#### c. `constraint_loss(weights, prev_weights, delta_limit=0.1)`
- **Purpose:** Penalizes excessive portfolio turnover and large deviations from a neutral weighting (concentration penalty).
- **Components:**
    - `turnover_penalty`: `F.relu(sum(abs(weights - prev_weights)) - delta_limit).mean()`. Penalizes changes in weights exceeding `delta_limit`.
    - `greek_penalty` (misnomer, acts as a concentration/max weight penalty): `torch.sum(F.relu(weights - 0.3), dim=1).mean()`. Penalizes individual asset weights exceeding 0.3 (30%).

#### d. `arbitrage_penalty(surface)`
- **Purpose:** Penalizes violations of no-arbitrage conditions in the generated surface (static arbitrage).
- **Calculation (Butterfly Arbitrage):** `F.relu(-dK).mean()`, where `dK = surface[:, :, 2:] - 2 * surface[:, :, 1:-1] + surface[:, :, :-2]` is the second derivative with respect to strike (convexity). Non-convexity (`dK < 0`) is penalized.
- *Note: Calendar spread arbitrage is not explicitly included in this specific function but could be added.*

#### e. `vix_replication_loss(surface, target_vix)`
- **Purpose:** Ensures the generated surface is consistent with a target VIX level.
- **Calculation:** `((approx_vix_sq - target_vix_sq)**2).mean()`.
    - `approx_vix_sq`: Approximated as `torch.mean(surface**2, dim=[1,2,3])`, a simplification where VIX-squared is the average of squared implied volatilities.
    - `target_vix_sq`: Squared value of the `target_vix` from mock data.

#### f. `dupire_residual(surface, strikes, maturities)`
- **Purpose:** Enforces consistency with the Dupire local volatility equation, a fundamental PDE in option pricing. This is a dynamic arbitrage constraint.
- **Calculation:** `((lhs - rhs)**2).mean()`.
    - `lhs = dT` (first derivative of surface w.r.t. maturity).
    - `rhs = 0.5 * strikes_normalized**2 * dK2` (where `dK2` is the second derivative w.r.t. strike).
    - **Strike Normalization:** `strikes` are normalized by a factor (e.g., 4500.0) before squaring to prevent the `rhs` from becoming excessively large and dominating the loss, ensuring better numerical stability.

### 2.4. Training Loop (`train_one_epoch`)

- **Data Fetching:** Generates a batch of mock data.
- **State Normalization:** The `spot` price component of `state_t_raw` is normalized (e.g., by dividing by 4500.0) to create `state_t_normalized`. This is crucial for stable training of `G` and `D`.
- **Generator Step:**
    - `fake_surface = G(z, state_t_normalized)`
- **Discriminator Training:**
    - `D_real = D(surface_t1, state_t_normalized)`
    - `D_fake = D(fake_surface.detach(), state_t_normalized)` (detached so G's gradients aren't affected here)
    - `loss_D` is calculated and `optim_D` takes a step.
- **Generator and Portfolio Training:**
    - `weights = P(fake_surface)`
    - `D_fake_for_G = D(fake_surface, state_t_normalized)` (not detached, to flow gradients to G)
    - Individual loss components are calculated: `loss_gan`, `loss_sharpe`, `loss_cons`, `loss_arb`, `loss_vix`, `loss_dup`.
    - `loss_G` (total generator loss) = `loss_gan + loss_sharpe + loss_cons + lambda_arb*loss_arb + lambda_vix*loss_vix + lambda_dup*loss_dup`.
    - `lambda_arb`, `lambda_vix`, `lambda_dup` are hyperparameters weighting the penalty terms. Their values have been tuned iteratively to balance the influence of different financial constraints.
    - `optim_G` (which optimizes parameters of both G and P) takes a step.
- **Return Values:** Returns all individual loss components, total losses, and debug values like mean generated surface IV and mean target VIX.

## 3. Key Features and Techniques

- **Conditional GAN:** The generator `G` is conditioned on market state variables, allowing it to produce contextually relevant volatility surfaces.
- **Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs):** Financial constraints (Dupire equation, VIX replication, no-arbitrage) are incorporated as soft penalties in the loss function.
- **Integrated Portfolio Optimization:** The portfolio optimizer `P` is trained jointly with the generator, learning to map volatility surfaces to optimal asset allocations.
- **Input Normalization:** Normalizing the `spot` price in `state_t` is critical for preventing exploding gradients and loss values, especially from the `dupire_residual`.
- **Generator Output Scaling:** The output of the generator `G` (after its internal `Softplus`) is scaled by a factor (e.g., 0.25). This directly helps control the average magnitude of generated implied volatilities, aligning them with realistic market levels (e.g., 0.15-0.25) and significantly improving VIX replication.
- **Careful Lambda Weighting:** The `lambda` hyperparameters for penalty terms have been iteratively adjusted to ensure a balance between the different financial objectives and the primary GAN objective. This prevents any single loss term from dominating the training process.
- **Strike Normalization in Dupire Loss:** Normalizing strikes within the `dupire_residual` calculation makes this loss term more robust to the absolute scale of strike prices and numerically stable.

## 5. Output

### 5.1. Console Output
During training, the script prints the following for each epoch:
- **Overall Losses:** Discriminator loss (`D loss`), total Generator + Portfolio loss (`G+P loss`).
- **G+P Loss Components (Unscaled):**
    - `GAN`: Generator's GAN loss.
    - `Sharpe`: Sharpe ratio loss (negative Sharpe).
    - `Cons`: Portfolio constraint loss.
- **G+P Penalties (Unscaled and Scaled):**
    - `Arb`: Arbitrage penalty.
    - `VIX`: VIX replication penalty.
    - `Dupire`: Dupire equation residual penalty.
    (Scaled values show the contribution after multiplying by their respective lambdas).
- **VIX Debugging Info:**
    - `Mean Fake Surface`: Average implied volatility across the generated surface.
    - `Mean Target VIX`: Average target VIX value for the batch.
    (These help monitor if the generated surfaces are consistent with the target VIX levels).

### 5.2. Plot
After training completes, a plot is displayed showing:
- **Top Subplot:** Overall Discriminator Loss and Total Generator+Portfolio Loss over epochs. Uses a 'symlog' scale to handle potentially large values or negative Sharpe components.
- **Bottom Subplot:** Individual (unscaled) components of the Generator loss (GAN, Sharpe, Constraint, Arbitrage, VIX, Dupire) over epochs. Also uses a 'symlog' scale.

This visualization helps in understanding the training dynamics and the behavior of different loss terms.

## 6. Potential Areas for Improvement / Future Work

- **Sophisticated VIX Replication:** Implement a more accurate VIX calculation from the generated surface instead of the current `mean(surface**2)` approximation.
- **Refined Arbitrage Penalties:**
    - Explicitly add calendar spread arbitrage constraints.
    - Potentially use different weights for butterfly vs. calendar arbitrage.
- **Generator/Discriminator Architectures:** Experiment with more complex or specialized architectures (e.g., convolutional layers for surface processing, attention mechanisms).
- **Hyperparameter Optimization:** Systematically tune learning rates, lambda weights, network hidden layer sizes, and latent dimensions. Consider using learning rate schedules.
- **Real Intraday Data:** Adapt the script to use real historical intraday market data for training and validation.
- **Advanced Portfolio Constraints:** Incorporate more complex portfolio constraints (e.g., sector limits, factor exposure limits) or alternative objective functions beyond Sharpe ratio.
- **Visual Inspection of Surfaces:** Add functionality to periodically save and visualize samples of the generated `fake_surface` to qualitatively assess their realism (e.g., smile, skew, term structure).
- **Alternative Activation for Generator Output:** Instead of scaling the post-Softplus output, modify the generator to use a scaled `Sigmoid` or `Tanh` as the final activation to directly enforce an upper bound on IVs.

## 7. Troubleshooting / Iterative Refinements History

Several key issues were identified and addressed during the development and refinement of this script:

- **Exploding Dupire Loss (Initial):** The `dupire_residual` loss was initially astronomically large (e.g., `1e18`).
    - **Cause 1:** Unnormalized `spot` price in `state_t` fed to the generator, causing it to output excessively large surface values.
        - **Fix:** Normalized `spot` price in `state_t` by dividing by 4500.0.
    - **Cause 2:** Large `strikes**2` term in the Dupire formula magnifying small errors.
        - **Fix (Iterative):**
            1.  Drastically reducing `lambda_dup` (e.g., to `1e-11`) to compensate.
            2.  **Structural Fix:** Normalizing `strikes` within the `dupire_residual` function (e.g., by dividing by 4500.0 before squaring). This made the unscaled Dupire loss much smaller, allowing for a more reasonable `lambda_dup` (e.g., `2e-4`).
- **Poor VIX Replication:** Generated surfaces had average implied volatilities much higher than implied by the `target_vix`.
    - **Cause:** Generator's `Softplus` output, combined with network weights, led to high IVs.
    - **Fix:** Scaled the generator's final output (after its internal `Softplus`) by a factor of `0.25`. This brought the `Mean Fake Surface` values in line with `Mean Target VIX`, effectively resolving the VIX replication issue.
- **Lambda Balancing:** Iteratively adjusted `lambda_arb`, `lambda_vix`, and `lambda_dup` to ensure that all financial constraints and the primary GAN objective contribute meaningfully to the training process without any single term dominating. This involved observing the magnitudes of both unscaled and scaled loss components.

These iterative refinements have led to a more stable and effective training process, where the model learns to generate financially plausible volatility surfaces while optimizing a portfolio.
